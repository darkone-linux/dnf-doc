The network hosts its own cloud, based on [Nextcloud](https://nextcloud.com).
My files, my calendar and my contacts are all in one place, reachable from my
browser, my computer and my phone. And everything stays **at home**, on our
server.

{/* t:p 4c9986396e70c97327c428260133ca32eddc5b9a0a21240747d3049db16ca3b0 */}
## What is Nextcloud?

Nextcloud is a storage space comparable to Dropbox or Google Drive, with three
differences that change everything&nbsp;:

- **Private**: my files are on the server at home, not with a web giant.
- **Complete**: files, calendar, contacts, photos, notes, link sharing.
- **Open**: browser, file manager or mobile app, my choice.

I sign in with my usual network account, without a new password to remember
(see [Manage my account](/en/doc/user-guide/usage/account/)).

| Access | What it does | When to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Browser** | The entire cloud, including shares and calendar | From any computer |
| **File manager** | Files stay on the server, opened like a network drive | View, drop off, without using my disk |
| **Synchronized folder** | A copy on my disk, kept up to date in both directions | Work offline, daily use |

<Aside type="tip" title="The two access methods add up">
There's no need to choose. The most comfortable way: link my account to see
everything, and only synchronize the folders I really need locally.
</Aside>

## From my browser

I open `https://cloud.domain.tld` and sign in with my network account. This is
the complete view&nbsp;: files, calendar, contacts, photos, shares, trash and
previous versions of my documents.

<Aside type="tip" title="The home domain name">
Throughout this page, `domain.tld` is the network's domain name. My
administrator gives me the exact address.
</Aside>

{/* t:p 66316fbba4554713acf452e3a4f05252d7c36864ac1e8ed81ce24d04e7302609 */}
## Connect my computer to my account

On a Linux machine on the network, two Nextcloud icons are installed&nbsp;:

| Icon | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Nextcloud Account** | Connects files, calendar and contacts to my computer. Just once. |
| **Nextcloud Sync Client** | Copies a cloud folder to my disk. |

I start with the first one.

<Steps>

1. I open **Nextcloud Account** from the app list. A small terminal window
   appears.

2. My browser opens on the cloud's sign-in page. I sign in with my network
   account, then click **Grant access**.

3. I go back to the terminal window. It shows my account, the server, and an
   **app password**.

</Steps>

That's it. Three things happen at once&nbsp;:

- A **Nextcloud (cloud.domain.tld)** entry appears in my file manager. It
  gives access to my whole cloud without copying anything to the disk.
- My **calendar** from the cloud appears in the Calendar app.
- My **contacts** from the cloud appear in the Contacts app.

<Aside type="note" title="The app password">
This password does not replace mine&nbsp;: it only opens access to the files,
and I can revoke it at any time from the cloud. I store it in my vault. It
will help me connect another device or another app to my account.
</Aside>

## Sync a folder to my disk

The sync client keeps a local copy of my files. I can therefore work
without a connection&nbsp;: everything syncs back when the network returns.

<Steps>

1. At login, the **Nextcloud** window offers to switch to the web browser. I
   click **Open**.

2. My browser opens on the cloud page. I sign in, then click **Grant access**.

3. The client asks for a **local folder**. It suggests `Nextcloud` in my home
   directory, which works perfectly.

4. On the same page, I choose **which folders to sync**. No need to download
   everything&nbsp;: I check what I use, and I can add more later.

</Steps>

A small cloud icon then appears in the top bar&nbsp;: it shows sync progress.

<Aside type="caution" title="The login link expires">
The authorization page is only valid for a few minutes. If the window has
been left open all morning, authorization will fail. I close the window,
reopen **Nextcloud Sync Client**, and try again&nbsp;: it's instant.
</Aside>

{/* t:p 8683f26b5ee3894ec07386444c02d0834a2c42f725a15af49f85176cf87d58bd */}
## Go back to my synced folders

The **Nextcloud** window only shows recent activity&nbsp;: it offers no
buttons, so I can't change anything there. Everything happens through the
cloud icon in the top bar&nbsp;: I click it, then **Settings**. The real
settings window opens, with my account and the **Add folder to sync** button.

This same menu also lets me open my local folder, pause syncing during a
shared connection, or quit the client.

<Aside type="tip" title="Answering later doesn't block anything">
If I skipped the folder step at first login, nothing is lost. The icon menu
takes me right back there whenever I want.
</Aside>

## Find my cloud in the file manager

After these two steps, my cloud appears in two places, and it's not a
duplicate&nbsp;:

- **Nextcloud (cloud.domain.tld)**, in the sidebar&nbsp;: my whole cloud,
  live. Nothing is stored on my computer, so I need to be connected to the
  network.
- **Nextcloud**, in my home folder&nbsp;: the folders I chose to sync. They
  are on my disk and remain readable offline.

A file dropped in the synced folder uploads by itself to the server, and
reappears on my other devices.

<Aside type="caution" title="Deleting means deleting everywhere">
A file deleted from the synced folder is also deleted from the cloud, and
therefore from my other devices. In case of a mistake, I recover it from the
cloud's **trash**, in the browser.
</Aside>

## On my phone

I install the **Nextcloud** app (Android or iPhone), enter the address
`https://cloud.domain.tld`, and sign in with my network account. The app can
also back up my photos automatically, if I want.
